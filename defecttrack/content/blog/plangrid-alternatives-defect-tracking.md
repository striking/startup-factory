# PlanGrid Alternatives for Defect Tracking in Construction (2026)

If you're searching for **PlanGrid alternatives for defect tracking**, you're not alone. Since Autodesk acquired PlanGrid in 2018 and folded it into Autodesk Build, thousands of contractors have been frustrated by rising costs, feature bloat, and a platform that no longer feels purpose-built for tracking defects and issues on site. Whether you're fed up with Autodesk's pricing model or simply need a tool that does defect tracking better, this comparison guide breaks down the best alternatives available in 2026.

We've evaluated each option based on what matters most to construction teams: ease of use on site, defect tracking capabilities, photo and markup tools, reporting, integrations, and pricing. No fluff — just honest assessments to help you make the right call.

## Why Contractors Are Moving Away from PlanGrid

Before diving into alternatives, it's worth understanding why so many teams are looking at **PlanGrid vs defect tracking tools** that are purpose-built for quality control.

### The Autodesk Build Transition

When Autodesk acquired PlanGrid, the standalone app that contractors loved was gradually merged into Autodesk Build — a broader construction management platform. While Autodesk Build is powerful, many users report:

- **Higher costs** — Autodesk's enterprise pricing model doesn't suit smaller contractors or subcontractors
- **Feature overload** — Teams that only need defect tracking are paying for project management, RFIs, submittals, and more
- **Steeper learning curve** — The simple, intuitive PlanGrid interface gave way to a more complex platform
- **Slower mobile performance** — The all-in-one app can be sluggish on older devices common on construction sites
- **Lock-in concerns** — Deep integration with the Autodesk ecosystem makes it harder to switch later

If your primary need is tracking defects, snags, and punch list items efficiently, a dedicated **construction issue tracking app** may serve you far better than a full-suite platform.

### 2. Procore

**Best for: Large general contractors already using Procore for project management**

Procore is one of the most widely used construction management platforms and includes quality and safety modules with defect tracking capabilities.

**Pros:**
- Comprehensive construction management platform
- Strong quality and safety modules
- Excellent integrations with accounting and scheduling tools
- Large user community and extensive training resources
- Robust reporting and analytics

**Cons:**
- Expensive — pricing is based on annual construction volume, which can be prohibitive
- Overkill for teams that primarily need defect tracking
- Complex setup and onboarding process
- Quality module may require additional licensing
- Better suited to general contractors than subcontractors

**Pricing:** Custom pricing based on annual construction volume. Typically $10,000+ per year.

**Verdict:** If you're already in the Procore ecosystem, their quality tools are solid. But adopting Procore just for defect tracking is like buying a truck to carry groceries.

### 3. Fieldwire

**Best for: Field teams wanting task management with defect tracking**

Fieldwire positions itself as a field management platform and offers task-based tracking that works well for defects and punch lists.

**Pros:**
- Strong field-focused interface
- Task management approach works well for defect workflows
- Good mobile app performance
- Plan viewing and markup capabilities
- Reasonable pricing for smaller teams

**Cons:**
- Not specifically designed for defect tracking — it's a general task tool
- Reporting can be basic for quality-focused workflows
- Limited automation for defect-specific processes
- Now owned by Hilti, raising questions about long-term direction

**Pricing:** Free tier available for basic use. Pro plans from $39/user/month.

**Verdict:** A solid middle ground between full project management and dedicated defect tracking. Good for teams that want task management alongside defect tracking.

### 4. iAuditor (SafetyCulture)

**Best for: Teams focused on inspections and compliance checklists**

iAuditor, now part of SafetyCulture, is primarily an inspection and audit platform that can be adapted for defect tracking through customisable checklists.

**Pros:**
- Excellent for structured inspections and audits
- Highly customisable templates and checklists
- Strong photo documentation features
- Good analytics and trend reporting
- Works across industries, not just construction

**Cons:**
- Not purpose-built for construction defect tracking
- Plan-based defect pinning is limited compared to dedicated tools
- Can require significant template setup to work for defect workflows
- Pricing escalates quickly with advanced features

**Pricing:** Free tier for small teams. Premium from $24/user/month.

**Verdict:** Best if your defect tracking is part of broader inspection and compliance workflows. Less ideal as a standalone **construction issue tracking app** for snags and punch lists.

### 5. Snagstream

**Best for: Residential builders and developers managing snagging processes**

Snagstream focuses specifically on the snagging and defect management process, particularly popular in the UK and Australian markets.

**Pros:**
- Built specifically for snagging and defect management
- Clean, simple interface for site teams
- Good photo and location-based documentation
- Designed for handover and defect liability period workflows
- Straightforward reporting for clients

**Cons:**
- More focused on residential than commercial construction
- Smaller company with less extensive support
- Limited integrations with other construction tools
- May lack advanced features for complex projects

**Pricing:** Project-based pricing. Contact for quotes.

**Verdict:** A strong choice for residential builders, but may lack the depth needed for larger commercial projects.

### 6. BIM 360 / Autodesk Construction Cloud

**Best for: Teams deeply embedded in the Autodesk ecosystem using BIM workflows**

Worth mentioning as an **Autodesk Build alternative** within Autodesk's own ecosystem — some teams opt for different configurations of Autodesk's construction cloud products.

**Pros:**
- Deep BIM integration for model-based defect tracking
- Powerful if you're already using Revit and other Autodesk tools
- Comprehensive document management
- Strong API for custom integrations

**Cons:**
- Same pricing concerns as PlanGrid/Autodesk Build
- Complex licensing structure
- Overkill for simple defect tracking needs
- You're not really escaping the Autodesk ecosystem

**Pricing:** Bundled with Autodesk Construction Cloud subscriptions. Custom enterprise pricing.

**Verdict:** Only makes sense if you're committed to Autodesk's ecosystem and need BIM-integrated defect tracking. Otherwise, you're trading one Autodesk product for another.

## Making the Switch from PlanGrid

Migrating away from PlanGrid or Autodesk Build doesn't have to be painful. Here are practical steps to make the transition smooth:

1. **Export your data** — Download all project documents, photos, and issue logs from Autodesk Build before cancelling
2. **Run tools in parallel** — Use your new tool alongside PlanGrid on one project before fully committing
3. **Train your site teams** — The biggest risk in switching tools is field adoption. Choose something intuitive
4. **Migrate active projects first** — Start with projects that have the most to gain from better defect tracking
5. **Communicate with subcontractors** — Give subs advance notice and simple instructions for the new platform
